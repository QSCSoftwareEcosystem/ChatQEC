## Analysis

### Noiseless Truth Table

Without any errors, the circuit prepares $\frac{|00\rangle + |11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$, so measuring both qubits always yields either $00$ or $11$ — the two outcomes are **always equal**. Therefore:

$$\text{DETECTOR} = M_0 \oplus M_1 = 0 \quad \text{(always, no noise)}$$

### What X errors do

The `X_ERROR(0.2)` flips each qubit **independently** with probability $p = 0.2$. There are four scenarios per shot:

| Error on q0 | Error on q1 | Effect on state | $M_0 \oplus M_1$ | Detector fires? |
|:-----------:|:-----------:|:----------------|:----------------:|:---------------:|
| No | No | $\frac{\|00\rangle+\|11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $0$ | ❌ |
| Yes | No | $\frac{\|10\rangle+\|01\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $1$ | ✅ |
| No | Yes | $\frac{\|01\rangle+\|10\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $1$ | ✅ |
| Yes | Yes | $\frac{\|10\rangle+\|01\rangle \to \|00\rangle+\|11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $0$ | ❌ |

The detector fires whenever **exactly one** of the two qubits is flipped. The probability of this is:
$$P(\text{detector fires}) = 2p(1-p) = 2(0.2)(0.8) = 0.32$$

So roughly **~3 out of 10 shots** should show the detector firing, matching our simulation results. The detector is **blind to correlated (both-qubit) X errors** — this is exactly the *hook error* / degeneracy phenomenon relevant to circuit-level noise models.[^5]

## Summary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `DETECTOR rec[-1] rec[-2]` | Computes $M_0 \oplus M_1$; should be 0 noiseless [^2] |
| Fires (= 1) | At least one unexpected error flipped the parity [^2] |
| Doesn't fire (= 0) | Either no error, or an **undetectable even-weight error** [^5] |

Detectors are the building blocks of syndrome extraction; their firing pattern is exactly what gets passed to a decoder like MWPM.[^2]
